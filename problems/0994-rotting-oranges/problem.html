994. Rotting Oranges Medium Matrix BFS
Key idea: Multi-source BFS. Put every rotten orange in the queue at the start, then process the queue one level at a time: each level is one minute. Rot a neighbor the moment you queue it, count the fresh oranges as you go, and if any are left at the end, return -1. O(m · n).

Problem View on LeetCode (opens in a new tab) ↗

In an m × n grid, 0 is an empty cell, 1 a fresh orange and 2 a rotten one. Every minute, each fresh orange next to a rotten one (up, down, left or right) becomes rotten. Return the minimum number of minutes until no fresh orange is left, or -1 if that can't happen.

  • 1 <= m, n <= 10
  • grid[i][j] is 0, 1 or 2.

Examples: [[2,1,1],[1,1,0],[0,1,1]] → 4; [[2,1,1],[0,1,1],[1,0,1]] → -1 (the bottom-left orange is cut off); [[0,2]] → 0.

Edge cases

Try these against your solution before reading the approaches.

Input Expected Why it's tricky
[[0]] 0 No oranges at all: nothing to wait for, so 0, not -1.
[[0,2]] 0 No fresh oranges at the start: 0 minutes. Don't count a minute just because the queue isn't empty.
[[1]] -1 A fresh orange with no rotten one anywhere.
[[2,1]] 1 Off by one: the last level of the BFS rots nothing new, so don't count it as a minute.
[[2,0],[0,1]] -1 Diagonal neighbors don't count: only up, down, left and right.
[[2,1,1],[0,1,1],[1,0,1]] -1 Most oranges rot, but one is walled off by empty cells.
[[2,1,1],[1,1,1],[0,1,2]] 2 Two rotten oranges spread at the same time, so each fresh orange rots from whichever is closer. Spreading from one rotten orange at a time and keeping the largest time gives 3.
[[1,1,2,1,1]] 2 The rot spreads both ways at once.
[[2,1,1],[1,1,0],[0,1,1]] 4 The classic bug: rotting oranges during the scan lets the rot spread several steps in one minute, and that version answers 1. Collect first, then rot.
10 × 10, all 1 except a 2 in one corner 18 The far corner is 9 + 9 steps away.
10 × 10 snake: rows of 1s joined by one gap at alternating ends, 2 at the start 54 The longest path: the rot has to wind through every row, so the simulation scans the whole grid 55 times (54 minutes, then one scan that finds nothing).

Approach 1: Minute-by-Minute Simulation

Do exactly what the problem describes. Each minute, scan the whole grid, collect every fresh orange that touches a rotten one, then rot all of them together. Stop when a minute changes nothing.

  • Collect first, then rot. If you rot in place during the scan, an orange rotted this minute can rot its neighbor in the same minute. On the example above that answers 1 instead of 4.
  • At the end, any fresh orange left can never be reached: return -1.
Time O((m · n)²) Space O(m · n)

Up to m · n minutes (54 on the 10 × 10 snake), and each one rescans all m · n cells. With at most 100 cells that's still tiny, so it passes, but most scans look at cells that can't change.

Approach 2: Multi-Source BFS Optimal

Only the oranges that rotted last minute can rot anything new, so keep them in a queue instead of rescanning. Start with every rotten orange in the queue: they all spread at the same time.

  • Process the queue one level at a time (for _ in range(len(queue))). Each level is one minute.
  • Rot a neighbor and lower fresh as soon as you queue it, so nothing is queued twice. Looping while queue and fresh stops before an extra, empty minute.
  • After the loop, fresh > 0 means some oranges were unreachable: -1.
Time O(m · n) Space O(m · n)

Every cell is queued at most once and checks 4 neighbors. This is the standard pattern for "spreads one step per minute from several places at once" problems.

In practice No shortcut

No standard library spreads values across a grid. The only built-in that helps is the queue itself: collections.deque in Python, std::queue in C++ and ArrayDeque in Java, all used in Approach 2.

Approach 2 is what you'd write. In Python, avoid list.pop(0) for the queue: it's O(n) per pop, while deque.popleft() is O(1).
