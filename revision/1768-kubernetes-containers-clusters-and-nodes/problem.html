Kubernetes › 1. Kubernetes Basics › Containers, clusters and nodes

Q. What is a container, and how does Kubernetes manage containers? A container is a lightweight, isolated app package with its dependencies. Kubernetes groups containers into Pods, schedules them on nodes, manages their lifecycle, and handles networking and scaling.

Q. What is the role of a Kubernetes cluster? A group of nodes running containers, managed as one system for deployment, scaling, and fault tolerance.

Q. What is the difference between a node and a cluster?

  • Node: a single machine (master or worker).
  • Cluster: all nodes working together under a control plane.

Q. What is a Pod in Kubernetes? The smallest unit. It runs one or more containers that share network (IP) and storage. Pods are ephemeral and managed by controllers.

Q. What is a Namespace, and how is it used? A logical partition that isolates resources, scopes access, and limits quotas for teams or projects.
