2.2 Choosing K (1/2) ML Algorithms

ML Algorithms › 2. Clustering: K-Means, Choosing K, Interpreting Clusters › 2.2 Choosing K

Elbow method. Track inertia (sum of squared distances from each point to its nearest centroid) as k grows. Inertia always falls as k rises, so look for the bend where gains become small. Trick: slide an angled ruler along the curve; where it touches the corner is the elbow.

Silhouette coefficient. How similar a point is to its own cluster versus other clusters, from -1 to 1 (higher is better). 1 = near the cluster centre, 0 = on the boundary, -1 = far away or misassigned (likely outlier).

Reading the silhouette plot:

  • Each cluster is a "blade"; each horizontal slice is one point, sorted by score. The red dashed line is the average score.
  • Good: blades bulge out (wide, rounded) and all poke past the average line.
  • Bad: pointy, narrow blades, a cluster that falls below the average, or negative scores at the bottom of a blade.
  • Iris: 2 clusters gives bulgy blades and the highest average; the average drops at 3, negatives appear at 4-5, and it recovers slightly at 6-7.
