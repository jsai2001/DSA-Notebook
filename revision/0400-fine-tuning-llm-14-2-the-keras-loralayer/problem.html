output = dense(x) + (x @ A) @ B: A is (input_dim × rank), B is (rank × units), both trainable; the wrapped dense layer is frozen. rank is the bottleneck width (default 4).

  • inputs @ A @ B is the low-rank update; original_output + lora_output is W·x + A·B·x; the original weights stay non-trainable.
  • Note: unlike the PyTorch version in Section 12.3, both matrices use random_normal here (no zero-init for B) and there is no alpha/rank scaling.
  • Lecture sketches (explore_model_layers, apply_lora_to_model, recursive_lora_replacement) say in their own comments that real layer replacement "requires more complex model reconstruction"; the notebook approach below is the working version.
